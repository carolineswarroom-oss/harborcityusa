RECORD DESCRIPTION

What is on file

In Harbor City, even a book that was never borrowed can be returned.

Dani Reed and her five best friends survived a receipt from tomorrow. Now they're trying to get back to normal.

Babysitting. Errands. Homework. Getting paid.

Their new club has a simple promise: Reliable. Safe. On record.

Then their first job takes them to an apartment near the lighthouse, where four-year-old Arlo Whitlock is terrified of bedtime stories.

Read the complete filed description

His mother's cat delivers a library book she's already thrown away twice.

Inside its cover, two impossible stamps wait:

RETURNED.

OPEN.

And the kitchen printer has an instruction of its own:

READ ALOUD.

The Harbor Six know better than to follow an order just because it's printed on paper.

But the trouble doesn't stay inside the apartment. Library books appear without catalog records. Due dates stop making sense. A harmless school reading program begins collecting something more valuable than homework.

And somewhere beneath Harbor City, somebody is keeping an account of days that don't officially exist.

Dani has a ledger. Renee has a plan. Tessa has questions the city would rather nobody ask. And the six girls have learned one very important lesson:

When a system changes the record, the only way to fight back is to keep your own.

THE RETURN STAMP is Book 2 of The Harbor Six Mysteries, an eerie, darkly funny middle-grade adventure about friendship, impossible dates, dangerous stories, and six girls who refuse to let paperwork decide what's real.

Because in Harbor City, nothing is ever truly closed. It can always be returned.
