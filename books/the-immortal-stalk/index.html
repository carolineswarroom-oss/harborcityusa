RECORD DESCRIPTION

What is on file

The valley calls it a miracle.

The boy calls it a problem.

After leaving the peninsula behind, the boy and the Builder descend through the fog into a hidden valley where everything seems to bend toward one impossible thing.

The rivers converge. The trees lean inward. Even the wind follows a strange, deliberate pattern.

And at the center stands a temple built around something that refuses to stop growing.

Read the complete filed description

An enormous living stalk rises through a roof that has been rebuilt again and again to accommodate it.

Pilgrims travel for days to witness the sacred Gift. Priests guard its mysteries. An entire village has built its faith, its economy, and its history around something nobody can explain.

But the boy is not interested in miracles.

He notices the water.

The pressure.

The widening foundations.

The repairs nobody seems willing to discuss.

And beneath centuries of worship, he begins uncovering evidence of something far more extraordinary than divine intervention.

A forgotten method. An erased beginning. A truth that has continued growing long after its maker disappeared from history.

The deeper he and the Builder investigate, the more dangerous their questions become. Because understanding the miracle threatens everyone whose authority depends upon keeping it mysterious.

Then comes Vale.

A girl who has been making her own observations, preserving her own records, and noticing the things everyone else has learned not to see.

With her aboard, the balloon becomes something more than an escape from the world below.

It becomes a way to bear witness.

And when the ancient hum returns, the three travelers begin to understand that the valley was never an isolated mystery.

It was evidence.

The Immortal Stalk is Book Two of Boy in the Balloon: Chronicles Through Time, a mythic speculative-fiction journey through forgotten engineering, buried histories, inherited beliefs, and the dangerous difference between preserving a miracle and understanding how it works.

Some miracles are discovered.

Some are maintained.

And some survive only because the world forgot who made them.
