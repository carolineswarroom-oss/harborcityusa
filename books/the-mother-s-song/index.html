HIS MOTHER TAUGHT HIM THAT SAFETY HAD A RHYTHM.

Two soft notes. A pause. A final turn carried through the trees.

Then the strangers came.

His mother was killed. His people fled. And the child who survived became the one person who could recognize the danger following them.

He could read pursuit in the smallest disturbances: a branch moving against the wind, an unnatural silence in the canopy, birds scattering where no birds should scatter.

Read the complete filed description

No one wanted to hear him.

When his warnings brought resentment instead of gratitude, his people cast him beyond the firelight.

But exile did not stop him from protecting them.

He survived in the treeline, watching over the people who rejected him. He left food where they could pretend the forest had provided it. He learned to communicate through birds that gradually began repeating the rhythm of his mother's song.

And without knowing it, he began creating something that would outlive them all.

A language without borders. A warning without a speaker. A memory that could fly.

Beyond the forest, the world was growing crueler.

Markets turned human lives into entries in ledgers. Chains followed trade routes. Men built power from fear, ownership, and the certainty that no one would interfere.

The exile decided to interfere.

What began as one child's desperate attempt to keep his people alive becomes a hidden network of watchers, makers, fugitives, and liberators.

But through every mile, every scar, and every life pulled from the machinery of ownership, one question follows him:

Can love survive after everyone who remembers its original voice is gone?

The Mother's Song is Book One of 505 Chronicles Through Time, a mythic ancient-world survival saga about exile, resistance, inherited violence, and the extraordinary ways love and knowledge survive across generations.
