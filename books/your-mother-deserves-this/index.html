What is on file

Your mother deserves a vacation.

Harbor Legacy Excursions has already decided whether she'll come home.

At 2:13 in the morning, Daniel Morrow is exhausted, worried, and running out of ways to help his aging mother.

Then an advertisement offers the perfect gift.

A beautiful coastal weekend. Comfortable accommodations. Attentive staff. Every detail handled by professionals.

Read the complete filed description

For once, somebody else can take care of everything.

Daniel books the trip.

His mother, Evelyn, happens to work for the company.

And she's just discovered something terribly wrong with its passenger records.

Eleven travelers. Eleven deaths. Eleven return tickets canceled before the people holding them died.

Evelyn has spent twenty-two years making sure older passengers get where they're going. She knows the difference between an unfortunate accident and an administrative error.

She also knows that paperwork isn't supposed to predict the future.

But Harbor Legacy insists everything is normal.

The cancellations are predictive logistics.

The missing passengers are routine adjustments.

And the company's increasingly aggressive interest in Evelyn's well-being is simply excellent customer service.

Then Evelyn discovers her own return transportation has been marked NO SERVICE REQUIRED.

With help from a retired school secretary who keeps copies of everything, Evelyn follows a trail of one-stripe luggage tags, invented relatives, impossible incident reports, and families whose private exhaustion has been transformed into authorization.

Because Harbor Legacy doesn't need anyone to wish their parents harm.

It only needs them to wish somebody else would take care of things.

And the company is very, very good at taking care of things.

YOUR MOTHER DESERVES THIS is a darkly funny, deeply unsettling speculative thriller set in the interconnected Harbor Pier universe. A story about aging, family, institutional power, and the terrifying difference between offering help and deciding someone is no longer needed.

The journey is a gift.

The return is not guaranteed.
